Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers to the budgeting questions every expecting parent asks.

How much does a baby cost in the first year?

BabyCenter's 2025 research puts the average at $20,384. Budget-conscious families land near $15,000; premium gear plus a full-time nanny can push past $50,000. Your biggest variable is childcare — see our 2026 average cost guide for the details.

How much should I budget per month for a baby?

Plan $1,100–$2,500 per month with paid childcare, or $400–$800 without it. Infant daycare alone averages $1,230/month nationally. Our cost calculator breaks it down for your situation.

What is the biggest first-year baby expense?

Childcare — typically 40–60% of the first-year total for working parents. A daycare center averages $1,230/month, home-based care $970/month, and a full-time nanny about $2,700/month.

How much do diapers cost per month?

Most families spend $60–$120 per month on disposables — roughly $70–$80 on average. Babies use 2,500–3,000 diapers in the first year. Cloth diapers cost $300–$800 upfront and can save $500+ over disposables.

How much does baby formula cost per month?

About $75/month for store-brand formula, $100–$150 for name brands, $150–$250 for premium/organic. Breastfeeding isn't free either — budget ~$30/month for pump parts, storage and nursing supplies.

Can I afford a baby on a tight budget?

Yes. Breastfeeding, cloth diapers, secondhand gear, and family childcare can bring the first year under $5,000. Try the calculator's budget options to see your own minimum, and read our methodology for where every number comes from.

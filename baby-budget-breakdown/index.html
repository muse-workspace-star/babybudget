The standard first-year split — what share each category takes, the three levers that move the total most, and worked examples at three budget levels.

The standard first-year model

These shares reflect how 2026 US survey data distributes first-year spending for a typical family. They add up to 100% — a sane starting point, not a rule. Use our budget allocator to apply them to your own total instantly.

CategoryShareWhat it covers
Childcare52%Daycare center, home-based care, or nanny
Gear & nursery13%Crib, stroller, car seat, nursery setup (one-time)
Feeding12%Formula or breastfeeding supplies, solids from ~6 months
Diapers & supplies8%Diapers, wipes, creams, changing supplies
Health & insurance6%Premium bump, copays, out-of-pocket birth costs
Clothing5%They outgrow sizes every few weeks — plan for it
Contingency4%Buffer for surprises — don't skip this

The three levers that move the total most

  • Childcare type. Family care ($0) vs. a full-time nanny (~$2,700/mo) swings the year by ~$32,000 — more than every other choice combined. Research options during pregnancy: quality infant spots have 6–18 month waitlists.
  • Feeding. Breastmilk (~$30/mo in supplies) vs. premium formula (~$250/mo) differs by ~$2,600 over the year. Combo feeding lands in the middle.
  • Gear tier. Secondhand basics (~$1,200) vs. premium brands (~$6,000). Babies outgrow gear fast — this is the easiest place to save without affecting daily life.
Rule of thumb: lock in your childcare plan first — it decides more than half the budget. Then pick your feeding approach. Everything else (gear, diapers, clothing) is optimization on the margins.

Common budgeting mistakes

  • Budgeting the gear, forgetting the months. The nursery is a one-time hit; feeding and diapers repeat 12 times. Multiply before you compare.
  • Using the sticker tuition. Daycare quotes often exclude registration ($100–$300), supply fees, and holiday closures at full tuition. Ask for the complete written fee schedule.
  • Ignoring the health premium bump. Adding a child to employer coverage raises the worker's share — KFF's 2025 survey puts the average family premium at $26,993, with workers paying $6,850.
  • No contingency. Growth spurts, ear infections, and a broken stroller are the norm, not the exception.
